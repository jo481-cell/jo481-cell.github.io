Secrets & Legends · Urban legend

Always one second too late

You cut every corner and cross the bridge faster than before. He is already there. What if the missing second was never yours to win?

← All legendsOctober 4, 2026Original game · Historical dossier

Illustration: Switch 2 Hyrule Field imagery, not a screenshot of the original Running Man race. © Nintendo.

The archive’s verdict · No normal-play victory

In the original game’s normal race, the Running Man reports finishing one second faster than your time. A better route does not unlock a victory reward.

Record 01

A race that looks like a promise

The challenge invites an ordinary assumption: a measured route should have a winning time. In the original Ocarina of Time race, the Running Man’s result denies that assumption. The game’s dialogue includes his claim to have finished one second ahead of Link.

That tiny gap makes the outcome feel almost within reach. As an editorial reading, it is easy to see why a player might try a sharper turn, a different route or one more attempt. The displayed difference suggests improvement could be enough even when the intended encounter does not offer a normal-play win.

Record 02

The seven-year claim

Zelda Dungeon’s report on Easy Allies’ Game Sleuth investigation describes a video claiming a victory through a seven-year wait. The investigation rejected it as evidence of an authentic solution. The report’s conclusion is that the ordinary race cannot be won; the apparent demonstration did not overturn that result.

This is a useful test for any secret tutorial: does someone independently reproduce it on the stated version under the stated conditions? A convincing ending alone does not prove everything that led to it. A video can show a result while concealing that the game was changed.

Record 03

What to do with the missing second

If you enjoy improving your route, the race can still be a personal challenge. Treat your own time as the goal. If you are searching for a hidden prize, this is where the archive recommends spending that energy on another discovery instead.

We are describing the original game’s normal race. We are not making a universal claim about every modification, exploit or future version. A changed outcome needs its own version label and evidence. The legend is worth remembering; the impossible promise is not worth repeating as advice.
